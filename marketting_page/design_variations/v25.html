Why M17

Everything a stepper wishes it were.

no. 1

All-in-one package

Motor, driver, motion controller, and encoder integrated in a single unit — nearly the same size as a NEMA 17 stepper with the same specs, no protrusions.

no. 2

One bus, endless motors

Daisy-chain any number of motors over one RS-485 bus and control them all from a single connection point.

no. 3

Closed-loop precision

Built-in encoder with a PID loop running at 32 kHz — and much better power efficiency than a conventional stepper.

no. 4

High-level commands

“Enable MOSFETs.” “Trapezoid move.” No timing-critical STEP/DIR signals to generate, ever.

no. 5

Protected by design

Integrated over-current, over-voltage, and over-temperature protection with comprehensive error handling.

no. 6

Drop-in mounting

Standard NEMA 17 mounting dimensions, 12–24V wide voltage range, and the same torque-to-weight ratio as an equivalent stepper.

Get Started

Your first move takes eight lines of Python.

Control from any platform — Mac, PC, Raspberry Pi, Arduino, or ESP32 — with nothing but a low-cost RS485 adapter between you and motion.

pip3 install servomotor
Read the docs →
first_move.py
import servomotor
from servomotor import M3

servomotor.open_serial_port()
motor = M3("X", time_unit="seconds",
                position_unit="degrees")
motor.system_reset()
motor.enable_mosfets()
motor.trapezoid_move(360, 2)  # one revolution in 2 s

Prefer C++? The “Servomotor” library is in the Arduino Library Manager.
