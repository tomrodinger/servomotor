Sheet 02 / 14  ·  Integration

Four systems.
One part number.

A conventional axis is a bill of materials: motor here, driver there, controller somewhere in a cabinet, encoder bolted on the back. The M17 is one item on the assembly drawing.

Available in four models — M17-60, M17-48, M17-40 and M17-34 — the series offers flexible options to match specific torque and size requirements while the control characteristics stay identical across the range.

A

Motor

Brushless, four sizes, the same torque-to-weight ratio as an equivalent stepper motor.

B

Motor driver

On board, inside the envelope. Over-current, over-voltage and over-temperature protection are integrated.

C

Motion controller

Trapezoid profiles, multiple operation modes and self-calibration run on the motor — not on your host.

D

Encoder

Feeds a closed-loop PID control loop running at 32 kHz. Much more power efficient than a conventional stepper.

Four heights on one square footprint. Pick the torque and the price the application needs; the command set, the bus and the libraries do not change between them.

Sheet 03 / 14  ·  Bus topology

One pair of wires.
Every axis on it.

Motors daisy-chain on a single RS-485 interface and are controlled from one connection point. No pulse-and-direction wiring, no controller card per axis, no timing-critical STEP/DIR generation on your host.

Sheet 07 / 14  ·  Getting started

Six lines of Python.
One revolution.

Install the library, name the motor, tell it to move. The trapezoid profile, the commutation and the closed loop are the motor's problem, not yours.

spin.pyPython
import servomotor
from servomotor import M3

servomotor.open_serial_port()
motor = M3("X", time_unit="seconds",
                position_unit="degrees")
motor.system_reset()
motor.enable_mosfets()
motor.trapezoid_move(360, 2)  # one revolution in 2 s
spin.inoArduino
#include <Servomotor.h>

Servomotor motor('X', Serial1); // alias 'X' on the RS485 UART

void setup() {
  motor.setPositionUnit(PositionUnit::SHAFT_ROTATIONS);
  motor.setTimeUnit(TimeUnit::SECONDS);

  motor.systemReset();
  delay(1500);  // let the bootloader window pass
  motor.enableMosfets();
  motor.trapezoidMove(1.0, 1.0);  // one rotation in one second
}

void loop() {}

Sheet 10 / 14  ·  Indicators & controls

Two LEDs.
Two buttons.

Everything you need to know about a motor without attaching a host — and enough control to spin it, close the loop, or calibrate it, standing at the machine.

Fig. 10 — Rear face Label · buttons · LEDs

Green LED — heartbeat and traffic

Flashes slowly as a heartbeat. Flashes quickly to indicate that the bootloader is running rather than the application. Lights up briefly while a packet is being received, so it flickers with communication on the bus.

Red LED — fatal errors

Flashes a certain number of times to indicate fatal error codes.

Reset button

Restarts the microcontroller and returns all state to defaults.

Test button

Spins the motor, four ways, by how long you hold it:

  • Brief press — spins one way.
  • More than 0.3 s, then release — spins the other way.
  • At least 2 s, then release — enters closed-loop mode.
  • More than 15 s, then release — runs a self-calibration.

NoteThe motor spins during calibration and must be able to turn freely. Remove any load first.
